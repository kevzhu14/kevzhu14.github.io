About

01

I am a Chemical Engineering MASc student at the University of Toronto and a graduate researcher at the Vector Institute, working at the intersection of machine learning and the chemical sciences.

My research uses deep generative models, graph neural networks, and Gaussian processes to make structure–property and structure–function relationships learnable — across protein binding subpockets, polymers, and environmental process data. I care as much about honest evaluation as about performance: uncertainty quantification, calibration, and knowing when a strong baseline simply cannot be beaten.

Before machine learning I trained as a synthetic and analytical chemist — photoredox catalysis towards threose nucleosides, combinatorial organophosphate libraries, and quantitative NMR. That wet-lab grounding is what I bring to modelling work, and to the classroom.

I am an aspiring educator and knowledge translator, and I actively seek teaching assistantships in chemistry and materials science, machine learning, data science, and Python programming. See my teaching experience →

Away from the desk I race for the Canadian National Dragon Boat Team (3× gold, 3× silver at the 2025 IDBF World Championships) and the Iron Dragons Paddling Club, and I spent two years as Co-President of the U of T Run Club.

Research

02

Machine learning for chemical and biological data, built on a bench-chemistry foundation. Filter by topic, or open an entry for detail.

Topic
  • Graduate Researcher Vector Institute · University of Toronto 2025 – present

    Four threads, one question: what makes a chemical or biological structure predictive of the property you care about?

    • LatentPockets. Mapping and identifying structurally unique protein binding subpockets to expedite target selection towards structure-based drug design, using variational autoencoders.
    • Anomaly detection & turbidity forecasting. Built a convolutional-network benchmark for an Ontario water treatment plant, finding that no architecture overcomes persistence (best CNN R² 0.70 vs. 0.80) and tracing the ceiling to a low target variance of 0.0015 NTU² against a ±0.02 NTU instrument noise floor.
    • Deep kernel learning for polymer properties. Benchmarked deep kernel learning regression (graph neural networks & Gaussian processes), fusing molecular graphs with process conditions across 6 datasets and 13 targets, and attributing structure- vs. process contributions via integrated gradients.
    • Polymer thermal conductivity. Built an XGBoost/KRR framework predicting from RDKit descriptors, reaching test R² 0.82 against a 0.61 linear baseline on 531 polymers, with bootstrap-ensemble uncertainty quantification.
    • VAEs
    • GNNs
    • Gaussian processes
    • XGBoost
    • Uncertainty
  • Researcher, Medicinal Chemistry Self-Driving Lab Acceleration Consortium May – Aug 2025

    Unsupervised learning at building-block scale, in service of generative drug molecule design.

    • Evaluated the efficacy of chemical featurization techniques in the implementation of a novel bitBIRCH clustering algorithm for data enrichment towards generative drug molecule design.
    • Employed unsupervised learning models on an Enamine chemical building-block dataset of over 1.4 million compounds.
    • Leveraged data science and cheminformatics Python libraries (RDKit, pandas, Matplotlib) in troubleshooting and modifying scripts.
    • Clustering
    • Cheminformatics
    • RDKit
  • Research Assistant, Taylor Group Prof. Mark S. Taylor · University of Toronto 2024 – 2025

    Independently developed a photoredox catalysis-based protocol towards the synthesis of threose nucleosides, achieving 47% yield across 5 steps — more than 20% greater yield than previously established methods. Research course grade: A.

    • Employed photoredox catalysis towards the dehomologation of ribonucleosides, excising the 5′-carbon to circumvent lengthy threose nucleoside triphosphate syntheses.
    • Explored 5′C excision strategies via photocatalytic reductive dehydroxymethylation (via the 5′-N-alkoxyphthalimide (NHP) ether), decarboxylation via the 5′-carboxylic acid–NHP ester, and decarbonylation via the aldehyde.
    • Photoredox catalysis

    • Total synthesis
