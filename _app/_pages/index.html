Kevin Zhu

Machine learning for chemistry, materials, and drug discovery.

MASc student, Chemical Engineering ・ University of Toronto ・ Graduate Researcher, Vector Institute

I am a Chemical Engineering MASc student at the University of Toronto, working at the intersection of machine learning and the chemical sciences. My research uses deep generative models, graph neural networks, and Gaussian processes to make structure–property and structure–function relationships learnable — across protein binding pockets, polymers, and environmental process data.

Before machine learning, I was trained as a synthetic and analytical chemist: photoredox catalysis towards threose nucleosides, combinatorial organophosphate libraries, and quantitative NMR. That wet-lab background is what I bring to modelling work — and to the classroom.

I am also an aspiring educator and knowledge translator, and I actively seek teaching assistantships in chemistry and materials science, machine learning, data science, and Python programming. See my teaching experience →

Currently

  • LatentPockets — mapping structurally unique protein binding subpockets with variational autoencoders, to expedite target selection for structure-based drug design.
  • Deep kernel learning for polymers — fusing molecular graphs with process conditions across 6 datasets and 13 targets, and attributing structure- vs. process contributions via integrated gradients.
  • Forecasting benchmarks for water treatment — anomaly detection and turbidity forecasting for an Ontario water treatment plant, and an honest account of why persistence is hard to beat.
